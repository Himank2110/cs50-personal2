sort1 uses: BUBBLE SORT

How do you know?: It took the most amount of time for all three random.txt files. From the final visualization in the Lecture, I know that bubble sort is the slowest of all three. It was also the fastest for all sorted.txt files, which indicates bubble sort (if no swap, exit) method. Hence having O(n²) and Ω(n).

sort2 uses: MERGE SORT

How do you know?: It was the fastest by a huge margin for all three random.txt files. From above mentioned visualization, I know that merge sort is the fastest method by far. Hence having Θ(nlog n).

sort3 uses: SELECTION SORT

How do you know?: Its time was in between sort1 and sort2, closer to sort1. From above mentioned visualization, I know that selection sort falls in between the other two methods. Also, it took nearly the same amount of time for unsorted50000.txt and sorted50000.txt, which indicates that the program used the same method for both txt files. Hence having Θ(n²).
